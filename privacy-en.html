A Life in Weeks · Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026 · Applies to iOS version 1.0

How we handle your data

A Life in Weeks is a time-keeping tool that runs entirely on your device. Your birthday, name, goal age and appearance preferences, card titles, text and target dates, and life chapter names, dates or age goals, the week anchor, week entry text, original week date ranges and per-entry RGB colours are used to calculate progress, draw cards and store week entries. All of it is processed on your device; the app never uploads it to a developer server.

Life chapters and week entries

The Life page shows the whole life and named chapters. A chapter can use fixed dates, or an age goal between 5 and 180; age-based dates follow your birthday, fixed dates do not. Each cell shows its date range, each week entry holds up to 2,000 characters, and can keep an RGB colour independent of the theme. The week anchor defaults to Monday and can be set to today or a chosen date; once saved it stays fixed instead of drifting daily. Changing your birthday never rewrites an entry's original date; changing the anchor files entries under the week of their original start date and keeps all of them. Entries outside the visible range remain in the list.

Local storage and widgets

Your profile, cards, chapters and week entries live in the app's storage on your device. The app and its widgets share storage through an App Group. Widgets currently show life progress or a card you chose; week entries and week colours are not shown there yet. Widgets can display your chosen content on the Home Screen or Lock Screen, so add them according to your own privacy needs.

Icon changes

Icon links are off by default. When on, the app picks a built-in icon according to life progress in 5% steps, updating when you open the app or change your birthday or goal age. The lit proportion on the Home Screen may let others infer roughly how far along you are. Turning it off restores the fixed icon. iOS may show an icon-changed prompt.

Third-party services and permissions

This version has no ads, no third-party analytics SDK, no cross-app tracking and no developer-provided cloud sync. It does not ask for location, contacts, camera, microphone, photos or notification access. App Store distribution and Apple system services are handled by Apple under its policies. When you visit this policy or the support page, the web host GitHub Pages may process ordinary access logs under its own terms; that is separate from the app uploading your personal data.

Exporting and restoring yourself

In Settings → Privacy and data you can export a full JSON backup containing your profile and preferences, cards, chapters, week entries, colours and the week anchor. You choose where the file is saved with the system file picker, on device or in a cloud drive you use. Exporting to a cloud drive yourself is not developer cloud sync; that storage is handled by the service you choose. Backups contain private content, so keep and share them carefully. A restore is checked and then needs a second confirmation; it replaces your profile, cards, chapters, all week entries and the anchor, so export your current data first.

Read failures and data retention

If week data is damaged or unreadable, the journal switches to read-only protection and related writes are paused so the original data is kept. A JSON export then keeps the unreadable raw bytes in the unreadableJournal field; it cannot be restored automatically as a complete backup and must be repaired first. Data is kept until you change, delete or erase it. System backups may include app data, and how they are made, restored and retained is decided by your device and Apple Account settings. The app provides no developer cloud backup.

Editing and deleting

You can edit your profile, delete cards, chapters or single week entries; deleting a chapter does not delete week entries. When no card data exists on first launch, six built-in samples with fixed IDs are created (two small, two wide, two large). Existing users are not given these samples automatically and their cards are untouched; they can tap Fill in default cards at the bottom of the cards page, which only adds samples whose IDs are missing, never overwrites edited cards and never deletes cards you made. Deleting every card normally keeps the list empty after a restart; only Erase all local data or Fill in default cards brings samples back. Erase all local data needs a second confirmation, deletes your profile, cards you created, personal edits to existing cards, chapters and all week entries and colours, resets profile and preferences to sample defaults, resets the week anchor and rebuilds the six original samples from code. Reset to defaults keeps cards, chapters, week entries and the anchor, but resetting the birthday can change the whole-life range and age-based chapter dates. The sample card list is shared with widgets; fixed IDs let widgets that chose a sample keep pointing at it, but if the UUID an older widget chose no longer exists, such as a deleted card you made, the widget still reports it as unavailable and needs a new choice. System refresh may lag. Erasing never deletes files you exported, cloud drive copies or existing system backups; manage those separately.

Children and privacy

The app collects no children's personal information for the developer and offers no social or public posting features. Avoid putting sensitive details into cards that you would not want shown on the Home Screen or Lock Screen, and keep backups containing week entries safe.

Policy updates and contact

If features or data handling change, this policy is updated with them.
