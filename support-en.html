A Life in Weeks · Help and Support

See the present through the progress ring, life chapters, week entries and Home Screen cards, and remember the weeks you have walked. Requires an iPhone with iOS 17.0 or later. Last updated: 10 November 2026.

Set your own scale of time

Open Settings, enter your birthday, and set Next goal to the age you want to watch, from 5 to 180. That age drives the progress calculation; it is not a prediction of lifespan. The number of cells in the whole-life view follows your goal age and is not fixed at four thousand.

Set up a chapter for this stretch

In the Life page, switch between the whole-life view and named chapters, then create or edit a chapter. A chapter can have a start date and a fixed end date, or an age goal between 5 and 180; age-based end dates follow your birthday, fixed dates do not. The end date is not included in the range, and the start must be earlier than the end. Deleting a chapter keeps its week entries.

Read week ranges, write entries and pick colours

Tap a week cell to see its exact date range, then save an RGB colour independent of the theme and a week entry of up to 2,000 characters. A saved custom colour does not change when you switch themes. Use Jump to a week to find a date, or open the entry list to view and search every week entry, including those outside the current whole-life or chapter range.

Does the week start drift every day?

No. The week anchor defaults to Monday; in Week start you can choose the natural week, use today as the start, or pick a specific date. A fixed date is saved, and cells step seven days forward and backward from it, so nothing drifts day to day. Changing your birthday does not rewrite an entry's original date. When you change the anchor, entries are filed under the week of their original start date; a cell may then hold several entries, all of which are kept and can be opened.

Create and edit cards

When no card data exists on first launch, the app creates six built-in samples with fixed IDs (two small, two wide, two large). Open Cards to add a card and choose its content, style and size; existing cards can be edited, duplicated, pinned or deleted. If you leave an unsaved edit, the app asks you to confirm. Existing users are not given these samples automatically and their cards are untouched; tap Fill in default cards at the bottom of the cards page to add only samples whose IDs are missing, without overwriting edited cards or deleting cards you made. Deleting every card normally keeps the list empty after a restart; only Erase all local data or Fill in default cards brings samples back.

Add a Home Screen widget

Touch and hold an empty area of the Home Screen to open the system widget gallery, then search for A Life in Weeks. You can add a life progress widget or My cards. After adding, touch and hold a card widget, choose Edit Widget and pick a card; you can add several widgets and give each a different card. The Lock Screen supports the life progress widget. Widgets do not yet read week entries and do not show week text or custom week colours.

Why has the widget not refreshed yet?

The app schedules a progress timeline at roughly half-hour intervals and requests an update after you save your profile or a card; the actual refresh timing is controlled by iOS. You can open the app and use Settings → Home Screen widget → Refresh widget now. If the card a widget refers to has been deleted, choose a card for it again.

Can the app icon change automatically?

Settings → App icon · Week's glow → Follow life progress is off by default. When on, the icon changes in 5% steps after you open the app or change your birthday or goal age. The iOS icon-change prompt is system behaviour and the app offers no silent bypass; turn the switch off to keep a fixed icon.

Themes and backgrounds

Settings offers six theme colours and three backgrounds: dark, white and system. Shared cards and pages follow these settings; cards for a specific age keep their own design.

Export a full JSON file and restore from a backup

Go to Settings → Privacy and data and choose Export local backup. The JSON contains your profile and preferences, cards, chapters, week entries, colours and the week anchor. You choose where to save it in the system file picker, on device or in a cloud drive you use; choosing a cloud drive yourself is not developer cloud sync, and the data is not uploaded to a developer server. The file contains private content, so keep and share it carefully.

Choose Restore from backup and pick a JSON file. After it passes checks a second confirmation appears. Tapping Replace and restore replaces your current profile, cards, chapters, all week entries and the week anchor; it is not a merge import, and anything not backed up is lost, so export your current data first. Cancelling or a failed check leaves local data unchanged.

What if week entries fail to load?

When reading fails, chapters and week entries switch to read-only protection and related writes are paused to keep the original data. Export a backup first rather than erasing. In this state the JSON keeps the unreadable raw bytes in the unreadableJournal field; a backup containing that raw data cannot be restored automatically and must be repaired first, and it does not mean the app has repaired or successfully read those entries. You can pick another complete backup and confirm to replace and restore.

Data storage and deletion

There are no accounts and no developer cloud sync. Data is processed on your device and may be kept by system backups. Reset to defaults resets your profile and preferences while keeping existing cards, chapters, week entries, colours and the anchor; resetting the birthday can change the whole-life range and age-based chapter dates, but never rewrites an entry's original date. Erase all local data needs a second confirmation and deletes your profile, cards you created, personal edits to existing cards, chapters, all week entries and colours, resets profile and preferences to sample defaults, resets the week anchor and rebuilds the six original samples from code. Fixed IDs let widgets that chose a sample keep pointing at it; if the UUID an older widget chose no longer exists, such as a deleted card you made, the widget still reports it as unavailable and needs a new choice, so this is not proof that every widget has been restored. System refresh may lag. Erasing does not delete files you exported, cloud drive copies or existing system backups; manage those copies separately.
